## Introduction to HIPAA

HIPAA was enacted in 1996 to:
- Improve portability and continuity of health insurance coverage
- Reduce healthcare fraud and abuse
- Enforce standards for health information
- Guarantee security and privacy of health information

### Key Definitions

- **Protected Health Information (PHI)**: Individually identifiable health information
- **Covered Entity**: Healthcare providers, health plans, healthcare clearinghouses
- **Business Associate**: Entity that performs functions involving PHI on behalf of a covered entity

## HIPAA Privacy Rule

### Purpose
Establishes national standards for protecting individuals' medical records and other personal health information.

### Core Requirements

1. **Notice of Privacy Practices (NPP)**
   - Must be provided to patients
   - Describe how PHI is used and disclosed
   - Explain patient rights

2. **Individual Rights**
   - Right to access PHI
   - Right to amend PHI
   - Right to accounting of disclosures
   - Right to request restrictions

3. **Uses and Disclosures of PHI**
   - Permitted uses: Treatment, payment, operations
   - Required disclosures: To individual, to HHS for compliance
   - Authorized disclosures: With patient consent

4. **Minimum Necessary Standard**
   - Use only minimum necessary PHI for the purpose
   - Limit access to PHI on a need-to-know basis

## HIPAA Security Rule

### Purpose
Establishes national standards for protecting electronic protected health information (ePHI).

### Safeguards

#### Administrative Safeguards
- Risk assessment
- Security management process
- Workforce training and management
- Incident response procedures
- Business associate management

#### Physical Safeguards
- Facility access controls
- Workstation use and security
- Device and media controls
- Disposal of electronic media

#### Technical Safeguards
- Access controls (unique user IDs, passwords)
- Audit logging and monitoring
- Encryption and decryption
- Integrity controls
- Transmission security

## Breach Notification Rule

### Requirements
- **Individual Notification**: Without unreasonable delay (within 60 days)
- **HHS Notification**: Within 60 days for breaches affecting 500+ individuals; annually for smaller breaches
- **Media Notification**: If breach affects 500+ individuals in a state or jurisdiction

### Breach Definition
- Acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of PHI in a manner not permitted
- Compromises the security or privacy of PHI

### Breach Assessment
- Risk assessment required
- Factors: Nature and extent of PHI, identity of unauthorized persons, likelihood of misuse

## Business Associate Agreements

### Requirements
- Must be in writing
- Establish BA's responsibilities
- Include termination provisions
- Require return or destruction of PHI upon termination

### Business Associate Responsibilities
- Safeguard PHI
- Report breaches
- Disclose PHI only as permitted
- Comply with Privacy Rule requirements

- [ ] Minimum necessary policies implemented
- [ ] Breach notification procedures established

### Security Rule

- [ ] Technical safeguards implemented
- [ ] Incident response plan developed

### Breach Notification
- [ ] Breach assessment process established
- [ ] Individual notification procedures

### Business Associates
- [ ] BAAs executed with all business associates
- [ ] BA monitoring processes
- [ ] BA termination procedures

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

| Violation Type | Penalty Range |
|----------------|---------------|
| Unknowing | $100-$50,000 per violation |
| Reasonable cause | $1,000-$50,000 per violation |
| Willful neglect - corrected | $10,000-$50,000 per violation |
| Willful neglect - not corrected | $50,000 per violation |

### Maximum Penalties
- $1.5 million per calendar year for identical violations

## Best Practices

1. **Conduct Regular Risk Assessments**: Annual or when significant changes occur
2. **Train Employees**: Regular HIPAA training for all workforce members
3. **Document Everything**: Maintain comprehensive records
4. **Review BAAs**: Regularly review and update business associate agreements
5. **Implement Security Measures**: Use encryption, access controls, audit logs
6. **Prepare for Breaches**: Have incident response procedures in place
